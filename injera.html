Injera

East Africa · Ethiopia

About this dish

Injera is a spongy, slightly sour flatbread that is the heart of Ethiopian and Eritrean meals. It is made from teff, a tiny and very nutritious grain, mixed with water and left to ferment for a day or more. Diners tear off pieces of injera and use them to scoop up stews called wat, so the bread is both the plate and the spoon.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups teff flour
  • 3 cups water, plus extra to thin the batter
  • 0.5 tsp salt

Cooking instructions

Click a step to open or close it.

Step 1: Make and ferment the batter
  1. Whisk the teff flour and water together in a large bowl until there are no lumps.
  2. Cover with a cloth and leave at room temperature for 24 to 72 hours, until bubbly and pleasantly sour.
Step 2: Prepare to cook
  1. Pour off any water that has gathered on top and stir in the salt.
  2. Add a little water if needed: the batter should be as thin as crepe batter.
Step 3: Cook the injera
  1. Heat a large non-stick pan on medium heat without oil.
  2. Pour in a thin layer of batter in a spiral from the outside to the centre.
  3. Cover and cook for 2 to 3 minutes, until the surface is full of small holes and the edges lift. Do not flip it.
  4. Let it cool on a clean cloth, then serve with stews.

Where it comes from
