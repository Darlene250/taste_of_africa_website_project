About Us

Why we celebrate African food, and where it comes from.

Our mission

Our mission is to promote and protect African food heritage by sharing its recipes, ingredients and stories with the world.

African food is often reduced to a few dishes, yet the continent is home to thousands of cooking traditions. This website is a small step towards showing that variety: every recipe comes with its history, its main ingredients and a video so that anyone can try it at home.

A short history of African cuisine

Ancient roots

Many of Africa's staple crops were first farmed on the continent, including sorghum, pearl millet, teff, yams and cowpeas. These grains and tubers still feed millions of people today.

Trade and exchange

Trade routes across the Sahara and the Indian Ocean brought spices, new techniques and ideas. Later, crops from the Americas such as maize, cassava, tomatoes, chili peppers and groundnuts became central to many African dishes.

A global influence

African cooks, farmers and food traditions travelled around the world, including across the Atlantic, and helped shape cuisines in the Americas and the Caribbean. Today, African chefs and home cooks are bringing their own cuisines onto the world stage.

Food as culture

In Africa, a meal is more than food. It is a way of welcoming guests, marking celebrations and passing stories from one generation to the next.

A food landmark: Jemaa el-Fnaa, Marrakech

Every evening, Jemaa el-Fnaa square in Marrakech, Morocco, fills with food stalls serving grilled meats, soups, fresh juices and sweets. It is one of Africa's most famous places to taste local food.
